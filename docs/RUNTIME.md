# Options, patience, deadline and observability

Moved from CLAUDE.md (#202): the HiGHS options the patch adds or reads, the patience gates, the wall-clock deadline and the `[Sequential]` / `[Heur]` / `[HeurSol]` log lines. The rules here bind exactly as they did there.

## HiGHS options added or touched by the patch

- `mip_heuristic_effort` — **vanilla semantics, vanilla default (0.05)**. This is upstream's B&B heuristic knob: `moreHeuristicsAllowed()` admits B&B-dive heuristics while `heuristic_lp_iterations < total_lp_iterations * mip_heuristic_effort` (plus an initial 10000-iteration offset). It gates RENS/RINS *and* `fpr_lp`: `fpr_lp::run` sizes each call to the remaining LP-iteration headroom of that envelope (converted at `nnz` effort-units per LP iteration, capped at `heuristic_effort_budget(nnz, mip_heuristic_effort)`) and charges all consumed work — reference-LP solves plus worker effort — back to `heuristic_lp_iterations`/`total_lp_iterations`, so fpr_lp competes with RENS/RINS for the same budget instead of drawing unaccounted work. **A patched binary at default options matches vanilla's B&B heuristic budget exactly.**
- `mip_heuristic_fj_effort` / `mip_heuristic_fpr_effort` / `mip_heuristic_local_mip_effort` / `mip_heuristic_scylla_effort` — one effort budget multiplier per **presolve** heuristic, each a double in `[0.0, 1e6]`; budget = `value * (nnz<<10)` — a multiple of vanilla HiGHS's own hardcoded single-thread FJ limit, so `effort = 1.0` means exactly one vanilla FJ budget and the patience option beside it is in the same unit. The ceiling of `1e6` exists so a calibration probe can hand a heuristic a budget that *cannot bind*, leaving the wall clock as the single stopping rule. Budget is derived in `run_sequential` from the model's `nnz`. They are **independent**: raising one heuristic's budget does not lower the others'.
  - **FJ's option sizes one worker's allowance**; the other three size a whole dispatch, which `make_budget` divides by N. The `budget_is_per_worker` flag in `kChain` is the only place that distinction lives (spelled out because `per_worker` in that translation unit already means `HeuristicBudget::per_worker`, a size_t budget rather than a flag).
  - Defaults are **searched and held-out validated** — `B'-mix-cheapest`, `bench/ablation_search/`: fj `0.3317`, fpr `3.161`, local_mip `3.2865`, **scylla `0.0` — disabled**, because it produced zero accepted incumbents in ~380 runs while dispatching on every solve. The per-heuristic measurement in `bench/ablation_effort/` (fj `0.5665`, fpr `12.2559`, local_mip `13.9607`, scylla `3.068`) is the *search centre* those domains were built around, not a shipped vector: each is the median budget that reached a dispatch's last incumbent improvement over the 233-instance mipfeas list at 16 workers, taken over the dispatches that *finished* improving (a barren dispatch has no knee; a still-improving one is right-censored). `tests/test_smoke.cpp` pins the shipped four, because they live in `apply_patch.cmake` where nothing else checks them. Read the cross-build caveat under Patience before comparing any of them with a number measured on a different build.
  - Note: LocalMIP's reported effort folds in the cold-start construction sweep (Phase B greedy sweep from `local_mip_construction.cpp`, charged in coefficient-access units to match `WorkerCtx::effort`) on dispatches where pool and incumbent are both empty; the cheap Phase A column-write loop is excluded from the normal-path total for unit consistency, but a small `ncol` charge is still reported on the degenerate `nrow==0 || max_effort==0` early exit.
- `mip_heuristic_fpr_lp_effort` — the dive-time `fpr_lp`'s budget, a double in `[0.0, 1e6]`, default **`0.0`** — i.e. **`fpr_lp` ships off**. **Not in the unit of the four above**, and not a multiple of `nnz << 10`: `fpr_lp` draws from upstream's RENS/RINS LP-iteration envelope and charges back what it spends, so its budget is zero-sum against those two rather than an independent allowance, and the option is its **share of that envelope** (`fpr_lp::dive_budget`). `1.0` takes the whole slice. **The `0` is measured** (Ablation C, `bench/ablation_fprlp/`): `fpr_lp` is capable — alone with RENS/RINS off it fires on 79% of a 49-instance set and yields accepted incumbents on 20% — but in the shipped chain it produced **zero** accepted incumbents across 49 paired runs at the campaign limit, with nothing separating on the metric in either direction (0.955, CI [0.87, 1.05]). It is dominated by the two heuristics it draws the envelope from. No share fixes that shape, so Ablation C records a decision not to sweep; the lever is a gate, not a budget. `0` disables the heuristic from a return above every counter read. One consequence for the harness: a config naming `fpr_lp` leaves it unzeroed but does not raise it off its shipped `0`, so such an arm additionally needs `--extra-options mip_heuristic_fpr_lp_effort=1.0`.
**Heuristic selection is the effort options, and nothing else.** There is no separate selector: `mip_heuristic_<name>_effort` at or below zero means that heuristic does not run, so all thirty-two subsets of the five are expressible as the zero-pattern of five doubles — the form the tuning search uses. A zero budget declines every piece of work a heuristic does, down to `ProblemView`-sized setup.
  - **`heuristics::entry_enabled` in `src/mode_dispatch.cpp` is the single place an option becomes a decision.** `run_sequential` filters `kChain` through it, and returns before `make_problem` — the shared CSC transpose, the most expensive setup in that function — when it selects nothing. A skipped heuristic emits **no `[Sequential]` / `[Heur]` line at all**, which is what "skipped" has always meant here, so at the shipped defaults a solve's presolve trace is three lines, not four. `fpr_lp` reads its own option in `fpr_lp::run`, because it is not a chain entry.
  - **`heuristics::any_enabled` (`src/mode_dispatch.h`) is the one export**, for the patched `printSolutionSourceKey`: it drops the group advertising our five solution sources when none of them can appear, keeping the printed legend byte-identical to an unpatched binary's. That header is inserted into HiGHS's own `HighsMipSolver.cpp` and `HighsMipSolverData.cpp`, both compiled at `CMAKE_CXX_STANDARD 11`, so it declares nothing a C++11 compiler cannot parse — same constraint as `fpr_lp.h`. `heuristics::kFprLpName` lives there too, as the single spelling of `fpr_lp`'s `[Heur] name=` tag; the four presolve names come from `kChain`'s own `name` field, so a name cannot drift from the trace it produces.
  - **Two gates, not one, and they are not redundant.** `entry_enabled` skips the chain entry at effort <= 0. `HeuristicBudget::disabled()` still guards what that cannot see: an effort small enough that `heuristic_effort_budget` floors the derived budget to zero while the option is above zero. `tests/test_effort_zero.cpp` covers both, the second through Scylla's `[ScyllaOverlap]` line at `effort = 1e-9`.
  - **Zeroing all five is the "our heuristics disabled" ablation, and that is all it is. It is not a vanilla baseline and must never be used as one** — the baseline is always a separately built unpatched binary. It runs **no FeasibilityJump whatsoever**: upstream's standalone FJ call site never fires on a patched build, so FJ is ours or it is absent. With `mip_heuristic_run_feasibility_jump=false` beside it, it is the pure patch-overhead configuration `bench/check_vanilla_equivalence.py` compares against an unpatched binary.
  - The one configuration that asks for something and then takes it away — a positive `mip_heuristic_fj_effort` with `mip_heuristic_run_feasibility_jump=false` and nothing else enabled — warns, because it is heuristic-free while being spelled like an "FJ isolated" row. `bench/run_benchmark.py` greps for that string; `tests/test_smoke.cpp` pins it.
- `mip_heuristic_presolve_only` — bool, default `false`. After the presolve chain completes, exit the solve **before the root LP**, keeping the incumbent. Reports `HighsModelStatus::kSolutionLimit`, which is what HiGHS itself assigns for a user-configured search-size limit and — unlike `kNotset`/`kInfeasible` — is *not* rewritten by `cleanupSolve`, which would otherwise stamp `kOptimal` on a solve that never computed a dual bound. So a presolve-only run reports `Dual bound -inf`, `Gap inf`, `Nodes 0`, `LP iterations 0`, CLI exit **1** (`HighsStatus::kWarning`), and a finite `Primal bound` with real incumbent display rows when the chain produced something. `run_benchmark.py` accepts exit 1, so these land in a normal `.log`. It is ~25x cheaper than a full solve and the only way to measure the chain without B&B diluting it.
- `mip_heuristic_fj_patience` / `mip_heuristic_fpr_patience` / `mip_heuristic_local_mip_patience` / `mip_heuristic_scylla_patience` — **a double in the same unit as that heuristic's effort option**, defaults **0.0 / 0.3372 / 3.1943 / 0.0** — **searched** (`B'-mix-cheapest`), so they bear **no fixed relation** to the effort beside them, and they land in three different regimes: FJ's gate is **off** (0 means no gate at all), FPR's is live at 0.107x its effort, LocalMIP's sampled *above* its clamp so `patience_threshold` applies `effort/4` = 0.8216 (a degeneracy of the searched domain, not a measured value), and Scylla's is moot because the heuristic is disabled. Nothing may assume `patience == effort / 4`. See the patience section below for scope and for what `0` means.
- `mip_heuristic_skip_submip_chain` — bool, default `false`. When true, `heuristics::run_presolve` returns first thing in a sub-MIP (`mipsolver.submip`), so the RINS/RENS sub-MIPs — which copy their parent's options — run no presolve chain (#178). The default runs it there, as before the option; turning it on waits for a primal-integral benchmark.
- `mip_heuristic_fj_restart` / `mip_heuristic_local_mip_restart` — int `0..2`, default `0`: the `RestartPoint` (`0` default, `1` best, `2` hybrid) a rebuilt FJ or LocalMIP worker of the presolve chain takes, mapped onto `HeuristicBudget::restart` through `kChain`'s `restart` member. `mip_heuristic_local_mip_restart_weights` — bool, default `false`: `HeuristicBudget::randomize_restart_weights`. Defaults are today's behaviour draw for draw; #201 measures the arms.
- `mip_heuristic_run_feasibility_jump` — upstream's own FJ switch, and the one pre-existing option the patch still reads. `false` disables FeasibilityJump, the same thing `mip_heuristic_fj_effort = 0` does; it is ANDed on in `entry_enabled` through `HeuristicConfig::enable_switch`, the one entry that names one. It is kept live rather than retired with the native call site so it does not become a dead option on a patched binary. Every effort zeroed plus this set to false is the pure patch-overhead configuration.

## Patience: absolute, and read off incumbent improvements

Every presolve heuristic stops early when improvement-free effort crosses a threshold, at two levels — a runner-level gate over the whole dispatch (`ContinuousLoopState::effort_since_improvement` against `HeuristicBudget::stale`) and a worker-level gate in `WorkerBudgetState`.

**The threshold is an option, not a fraction of the budget.** `mip_heuristic_<name>_patience`, read through `kChain`. A threshold derived from the budget cannot bound over-budgeting: doubling the budget doubles the tolerance, so the gate never fires relatively sooner. The four are **not comparable across heuristics**, because only each heuristic knows what its own effort counter counts. **`0` means no gate at all** — unbounded, returned ahead of the clamp — not "give up immediately"; that is what lets a probe measure a heuristic with nothing stopping it.

`patience_threshold(nnz, per_base, budget)` in `heuristic_common.h` applies one, **clamped to `budget / kPatienceCeilingDivisor`, a quarter, not to `budget`**: a patience at or above the ceiling fires exactly at exhaustion, which is behaviourally identical to no gate and reported nowhere — and that is the *common* case for an honestly measured value, since the measured p95 exceeds the ceiling on three of the four heuristics. A quarter is FJ's own shape and is exactly where all four shipped defaults sit. `run_sequential` sizes the runner-level gate (multiplying by the worker count for FJ, whose option's scope is per-worker like its effort option) and `make_budget` divides it by N into `worker_stale`. **Scylla is the one documented deviation**: it takes the dispatch-level `stale` as its worker threshold, because its per-worker counter is charged the PDLP cost already divided by N.

**The improvement signal is an incumbent improvement, not a pool acceptance.** The pool keeps a top-K, so a heuristic that merely beats its own worst entry would reset staleness forever — measured over 233 instances at 16 workers, FPR earns ~3.3M pool acceptances against 590 incumbent improvements, and a patience calibrated on improvements cannot be spent against a gate that resets on acceptances. So `offer` returns **two** facts (`SolutionSink::OfferResult{accepted, improved_incumbent}`, `[[nodiscard]]`): gates read `improved_incumbent`, decided inside `SolutionPool`'s own lock against the best objective the pool has ever accepted — the only race-free place, since a worker may not read a solver field — and a **monotone watermark** rather than `entries_.front()`, which the diversity-replacement path can evict, degrading the pool's best where the solve's never degrades. `accepted()`, `[Heur] found` and `[HeurSol] accepted` keep their pool-acceptance meaning, because `bench/parse_highs_log.py` and external tooling depend on it. **Returning a struct rather than repurposing the bool is deliberate**: it makes every gate site name which fact it reads.

**`CMakeLists.txt` puts `-Werror=unused-result` on every library and test target of ours**, without which a re-drop of that result is only a warning — nothing here passes `-Werror` and the clang-tidy gate cannot see `clang-diagnostic-*`, since `.clang-tidy` opens with `-*`. Keep that flag on our targets and off HiGHS's. The two deliberate discards (`fpr_lp`, LocalMIP's cold-start publish) are spelled `static_cast<void>` with a reason.

**Only the gate's operand is the incumbent notion** — LocalMIP's `best_objective_`/`steps_since_improvement_` and FJ's internal `effortSinceLastImprovement` are the local search's own bookkeeping and still use the local notion. **FJ's worker-level gate is the one exception**: it reads FJ's own `effortSinceLastImprovement` alone (#191), since before a first solution the incumbent notion retires FJ however fast its violations fall; FJ's runner-level gate reads `improved_incumbent` like the others'.

**Scylla has a granularity floor no constant can cross**: one attempt charges a whole PDLP solve (`iters x nnz`), several times the ceiling, and `attempt_cap` does not govern a solve once started. Do **not** try to fix that by clamping `attempt_cap` — measurably ineffective.

**Caveat when comparing effort numbers across builds.** The four effort defaults and both patience columns are quantiles on *charged effort* and on *incumbent improvements*. Landed changes have moved how much effort a unit of real work charges (the in-tree repair's per-refuted-node scan, on FPR / Scylla / `fpr_lp` alike; the uncapped Phase 3; the epsilon route's cheaper pump iteration; `presolve=off` on the shared PDLP instance) and how a heuristic searches at all (FJ's two corrections, RepairSearch's jump and disjunction, the pump advancing on a failed rounding). **Any number quoted from a run older than the code in front of you is on a different axis** — re-measure rather than reconcile.

## The wall-clock deadline

**The deadline is bounded by a polling interval, not by an attempt.** `ExecutionContext` exposes two predicates, and which one a caller may use is a thread-safety fact, not a preference:

- **`past_deadline()`** is `deadline.expired()` — in the HiGHS adapter the solver's own clock against `time_limit` — or'd with the caller's `ExecutionContext::stop` flag, which the adapter leaves null and `run_until_stopped` callers set (#171). `HighsTimer::read()` is `const` and writes nothing for the solve clock, and the flag is a relaxed load, so **any worker thread may call it** — that is what lets every heuristic poll the deadline, and a caller's stop, from inside its own inner loop.
- **`terminated()`** first calls the context's `terminator` callable, which `make_exec` binds to `terminatorTerminated()` — and that *writes* `mipsolver.termination_status_` when a terminator is attached — so it is **seat-holder-only** via `ContinuousLoopState`. `run_sequential` also calls it between heuristics, where every parallel region has joined. **There is no exception to this rule.**

Cadences: FJ per upstream callback (`CALLBACK_EFFORT` = 500000 effort units); LocalMIP every `kTermCheckWork` counted units of `WorkerCtx::effort` — **work, not steps**, because a feasible-mode step calls `WorkerCtx::full_recheck` every `kFeasibleRecheckPeriod` steps and that charges one `nnz`, so the wall time a fixed step count buys scales with the model; FPR per inner attempt, every `kDeadlinePollNodes` DFS nodes, every RepairSearch node, every `repair_walk` step, *and* every `kPropagateDeadlinePollWork` counted units inside a propagation fixpoint; Scylla per pump iteration. The runner polls unconditionally on every iteration too (`ContinuousLoopState::should_stop`), which stops a worker that ended its attempt *on* the deadline from spinning on empty attempts.

`Deadline` (`src/deadline.h`) is the poll in the form a *sub-algorithm* takes it — a `const HighsTimer*` and a limit, null when the limit is infinite — for the layers below a runner that have no `ExecutionContext` (`fpr_core` is reached from `FprWorker`, `ScyllaWorker` and `fpr_lp`). **`deadline_of(mipsolver)` is the only way to build one from the solver, and `make_exec` builds `ExecutionContext::deadline` with it**, so a sub-algorithm cannot stop against a different clock or limit than its runner. Two callers need the *scalar* remaining time to hand a sub-solver rather than the predicate, and both read it off a `Deadline`: `ContestedPdlp` inside its mutex, and `fpr_lp`'s reference LP solves through `Deadline::remaining()`. **Don't inline `time_limit - timer_.read()` as a second spelling** — the same clock read in a file whose caller then cannot tell an expiry from a failed solve.

**The sequential dispatch setup is deadline-gated too**, and its cost is independent of every option: `fpr::precompute_var_orders` (8 orders), `ContestedPdlp` construction plus `scylla::precompute_config_var_orders` (5 orders), and `fpr_lp`'s `build_setup` (10 orders + 2 reference LP solves) all run before any worker exists and can each take tens of seconds on a large model. Each checks the deadline between orders and returns with an incomplete table rather than finishing the list, reporting `abandoned_setup=1`.

Two things this does **not** do: it does not un-truncate the chain — a heuristic that legitimately spends the whole limit still leaves its successors nothing, since `run_sequential` skips a heuristic once `exec.terminated()` — and it does not remove Scylla's floor of one whole PDLP solve. It also narrows reproducibility: a run whose deadline binds is wall-clock dependent even at `threads=1`, so a high-effort measurement run needs a limit its chain does not reach.

**Residual floors** (all in `docs/PARAMETERS.md`, "What bounds the deadline's tightness"): one LocalMIP local-search step plus `kTermCheckWork` counted units; `kPropagateDeadlinePollWork` counted units plus one row inside a propagation fixpoint; one `repair_walk` step — O(row degree x column degree) plus an O(`nrow`) rescan on a soft restart, which on a model with a dense row exceeds a propagation poll and makes it FPR's coarsest unit; one `compute_var_order`, the largest of them, scaling with the model into seconds on a large instance; one PDLP solve including the wrapped `Highs::run`'s own LP setup, plus `ContestedPdlp`'s own LP build, which is ahead of the first deadline check; and FJ's worker construction.

## Observability

At `log_dev_level=3` `EffortLedger::book` (`src/effort_ledger.cpp`) emits one line per presolve-chain heuristic per solve, plus one per `fpr_lp` dive dispatch. `bench/parse_highs_log.py` parses all of them.

- `[Sequential] heur=<name> effort=<N> wall_ms=<X> effort_per_ms=<R>` — **positional**, because external tooling reads it. Keep it alongside `[Heur]`.
- `[Heur] name=<n> phase=<presolve|dive> start_s=<S> end_s=<E> effort=<N> wall_ms=<X> effort_per_ms=<R> found=<0|1> nnz=<Z> abandoned_setup=<0|1>` — **key=value**, same emission site. It carries what `[Sequential]` cannot: *when* the heuristic ran on the solver clock, which side of the patch boundary it ran on, whether it produced anything, and whether it ran at all. This is the line a per-heuristic budget calibration reads.
  - `nnz` comes from `mipdata->ARindex_.size()` — the post-presolve MIP matrix the effort and patience options are denominated in. It exists because **`log_dev_level=3` suppresses the model header a parser would otherwise read**: the one-line `MIP <name> has ... nonzeros;` form is absent from every level-3 log. The level-3 `Rows/Cols/Nonzeros` block is *not* a substitute — it is the pre-presolve model.
  - `abandoned_setup` distinguishes a heuristic that searched and produced nothing from one that **never searched**. **Three log shapes must stay distinguishable**: no `[Heur]` line at all is a killed run, `abandoned_setup=1` is a setup bail, and the field absent or `0` is a dispatch that ran. It is emitted unconditionally on **both** the `charge_presolve` and `charge_dive` paths, so an *absent* field means one thing only — a log written before the field existed — and `HeuristicSample.abandoned_setup` is `None` there rather than `False`, since the distinction is unobservable on such a log rather than known-absent.
  - **There are two channels from a bail site to the ledger, and which one a heuristic uses follows from whether it books itself.** The four presolve heuristics are on the shared runner contract, so theirs is its return type — `DispatchOutcome` (effort + flag) in `heuristic_context.h`, booked by `run_sequential`. `fpr_lp` books its own dispatch, so its bail reaches the line as an explicit `charge_dive` argument and by nothing else. **That argument is deliberately not defaulted**, so a future dive-shaped caller cannot inherit `false` without considering the question.
  - **Never infer the bail from `effort == 0` plus an expired deadline**, in a log or in process: a dispatch entered with a millisecond left constructs its workers and exits at zero effort without ever bailing in setup. Only the bail site knows, which is why the flag is returned rather than reconstructed.
- `[HeurSol] name=<n> dispatch=<i> worker=<w> effort_at=<E> wall_ms=<X> obj=<O> accepted=<0|1>` — **key=value**, one per **accepted** `SolutionSink::offer`, at `log_dev_level=3` only. It gives the intra-dispatch view `[Heur]` cannot: productive effort (charged effort at the last accepted offer), stale effort, and the inter-acceptance gap distribution that calibrates a patience. It is narrowed to acceptances because the raw offer rate reaches ~11k/s on a small model — tens of MB per run for lines no consumer reads. The `accepted` field stays in the format (the parser requires it, and older logs carry both values); anything wanting a raw offer rate wants a counter in `[Heur]`, not a line per offer.
  - `dispatch` is a **process-global** counter — unique per dispatch, never dense or zero-based per solve — so group on `(name, dispatch)` and bind to the closing `[Heur]` line via `HeurSolSample.heur_index`, which is **the supported binding**; re-deriving it is how three of four heuristics silently vanish. `effort_at` is monotone only within a `(name, dispatch, worker)` triple, and is that heuristic's own unit.
- The ledger times against HiGHS's own solver clock, so `[Heur]` windows share an origin with HiGHS's display-line time column. That clock is **not** monotonic — `HighsTimer` bottoms out in `high_resolution_clock`, which libstdc++ aliases to `system_clock` — so a wall-clock step can produce a negative `wall_ms`. The bench regex accepts the sign so such a sample surfaces instead of silently vanishing.

**`effort_per_ms` measures *charged* effort, not useful work**, so it is **not a valid before/after metric** for a change that alters how much effort a unit of real work charges — only for comparing heuristics against each other at fixed code. Removing redundant work usually *lowers* it: an incremental-structures build can report a 2.8x rate loss on a 10x throughput gain, because the naive path charged a full `nnz` recheck it did not need. **Measure real work units** (LocalMIP steps, FPR DFS nodes per ms) when A/B-ing a hot-path change. Keep the four presolve heuristics and `fpr_lp` apart when comparing rates: they draw from different budgets. `effort_per_ms` is also a throughput, and Scylla scales sublinearly in workers (PDLP mutex) where FPR/LocalMIP scale near-linearly, so the worker-count factor does not cancel in cross-heuristic ratios — state the worker count with any such ratio.

**The presolve chain is ~4% of a solve, and that number is what retires a performance issue.** At shipped defaults the whole four-heuristic chain costs ~1.07 s on `50v-10` and ~1.73 s on `roll3000`, against a 43.6 s optimal solve of the latter — ~0.25% at a 600 s campaign limit — and it is *budget*-bound there rather than clock-bound, so a faster implementation does not search more, it hands the difference back to B&B. One FPR presolve dispatch is ~98 ms. One hotspot larger than anything on the retired perf list is deliberately unfixed, recorded so the next profile does not re-derive it: ~15% is `clock_gettime` from deadline polling. `SolutionPool`'s diversity scan iterates the integer index list with the rounding inlined and stops at the first entry too close (#195); re-profile before quoting its share. (Shares from a sampling profiler at 500 Hz, `perf` being blocked by `perf_event_paranoid` here; read them to one significant figure.) **Before filing or accepting the next micro-optimization, price it against the 4%**: the bar is not "is this redundant work" but "is this worth more than the re-baseline and the verification it forces".
