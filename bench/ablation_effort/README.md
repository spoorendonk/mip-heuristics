# Effort and patience, measured

The four presolve heuristics' effort budgets and patience thresholds, derived
from the #113 calibration probe rather than inherited. Everything in this
directory is regenerated by one command from a probe results tree:

```bash
bench/run_presolve_probe.sh preprobe next 12   # the measurement (~8 h, 233 instances x 4 arms)
bench/derive_from_probe.sh                     # every file here
```

Same tree plus same seed reproduces every file byte for byte; none carries a
timestamp.

**These are the shipped defaults.** They were adopted into
`third_party/highs_patch/apply_patch.cmake` (PATCH_VERSION 18) on 2026-09-06,
which is what this measurement existed to decide. The accounting comment
beside them in that file states the same derivation; `docs/PARAMETERS.md` has
the per-option entries.

## The measurement

Taken **2026-09-05/06** on a 16-core / 32-thread machine (HiGHS defaults its
worker count to half the hardware threads, so **16 workers**), CPU build.

Each heuristic ran **alone** — `mip_heuristic_suite=<one>`, not the chain —
presolve-only, over all 233 PLATO `mipfeas` instances at one seed, capped at
30 s, with its effort option at the `1e6` ceiling and its patience at `0`.
Those two settings are the whole design:

* effort `1e6` means the budget **cannot bind** on any model;
* patience `0` means **no gate at all** (not "give up immediately");

so **the wall clock is the single stopping rule, identical for every heuristic
on every instance**. That is checked rather than assumed:

```
probe check    932/932 presolve-only, 932/932 patched
budget check   881/881 traced dispatch(es) clock-bound, 0 budget-bound
               0 .log.err, 0 TIMEOUT kills, 0 abandoned_setup
```

Running each **alone** matters: `run_sequential` is sequential, so at an
unbindable budget the first heuristic takes the entire cap and a chained probe
would report the other three as having produced nothing. Membership in the
informative set below is the *union* over the four singles, which dominates
every subset — a heuristic that cracks an instance inside some mix also cracks
it running alone, earlier and with the whole cap to itself.

Cost: 932 runs in **8.0 h**, plus a 233-run FJ re-run in **2.0 h** after #163
(below).

## What was derived, and how

| heuristic | effort | patience | scope | knee rests on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fj | **0.5665** | **0.141625** | per worker | 144 finished |
| fpr | **12.2559** | **3.063975** | per dispatch | 139 finished |
| local_mip | **13.9607** | **3.490175** | per dispatch | 158 finished |
| scylla | **3.0680** | **0.7670** | per dispatch | 130 finished |

### effort = the p50 of the yield knee

Every `[HeurSol]` line stamps an accepted solution with the charged effort at
which it arrived, so **one clock-bound dispatch is a whole cumulative-yield
curve**. That dispatch's *knee* is the effort at its **last incumbent
improvement** — spend beyond it bought nothing on that instance. The default is
the **median knee over the dispatches that finished improving**.

Three choices, each with a reason:

* a **barren** dispatch (no improvement at all) has no knee. It is excluded,
  because it poses a *cost* question — answered by patience — not a budget one.
* a dispatch **still improving** when the cap fired is **right-censored**: its
  true knee is larger than what it was seen to spend, so counting it as if the
  cap were its knee would bias the estimate upward.
* the **median**, not a tail quantile. The p90 is driven by the still-improving
  dispatches, so it measures the cap rather than the heuristic.

The report also prints an upper end — the same quantile with the censored
dispatches carried as censored — which brackets the answer:

```
fj          0.5665 ..  6.6125
fpr        12.2559 .. 93.7124
local_mip  13.9607 .. 50.8226
scylla      3.0680 .. 17.8144
```

Read the shipped value as *a sensible centre to search around*, not as the
optimum: it is the starting point for #107's joint search, which is what #113
always intended it for.

### patience = min(p95 of the inter-improvement gaps, effort / 4)

The **p95 is a retention claim**: give up after this much improvement-free
effort and at most 5% of the improvements that would ever arrive are cut off.
The **clamp is a cost claim**: a barren dispatch spends *exactly* the patience
and nothing else, and 29–40% of dispatches are barren, so the threshold has to
stay well under the ceiling. The divisor is `kPatienceCeilingDivisor` = 4,
which is FJ's own historical shape.

**The clamp binds on all four.** The raw p95 waits are:

| heuristic | measured p95 wait | ceiling (effort/4) | shipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| fj | 2.46 | 0.141625 | **0.1416** |
| fpr | 5649.87 | 3.063975 | **3.0640** |
| local_mip | 4.93 | 3.490175 | **3.4902** |
| scylla | 18.96 | 0.767 | **0.7670** |

So every shipped patience is `0.25 x` the effort beside it, and the honest
reading is *"for three of these four, the measurement says wait longer than
your entire budget"*. LocalMIP is the only one whose measured wait is within
striking distance of its own ceiling fraction. **Raising the divisor is the one
knob here worth an ablation arm.**

Note the direction of that finding. The p95 is a *retention* statement and the
clamp is a *cost* statement, and on three of four heuristics they disagree by
orders of magnitude — FPR's measured wait is 5650 against a ceiling of 3.06,
a factor of 1800. So on those three the shipped value is **not** the
measurement: it is the cost bound, and the measurement only tells us the
retention loss from applying it is larger than 5%. Anyone reading these as
"the calibrated patience" is reading them wrong. What is calibrated is the
*effort*; the patience is a quarter of it, and #113's contribution on that
axis is the negative result that the honest p95 is unaffordable.

### Why both columns ship at four decimal places

The patience is the quarter **to rounding**, not exactly: `0.5665 / 4 =
0.141625`, shipped as `0.1416`.

Four decimals is the precision the measurement supports. Each effort is a
median over 130-160 dispatches whose per-instance spread is several percent,
so a fifth or sixth digit asserts accuracy that is not there — which matters
because these numbers are quoted in a write-up.

An earlier revision shipped the patience column as the *exact* quotient
(`0.141625`), so the two columns disagreed in precision. That existed only so
`tests/test_smoke.cpp` could assert `patience == effort / 4` with `==` —
values bent to satisfy an assertion rather than the reverse. The assertion was
also wrong in principle: `patience_threshold` computes
`min(p95, effort/4)`, and that equals the quarter **here** only because every
measured p95 came out above the ceiling. A future calibration finding a p95
*below* it would legitimately ship a smaller patience, and an exact test would
then fail on correct values. The test now checks the relation to within 0.1%,
which still catches what it guards: an effort default moved without its
patience (a 4x error), or a divisor typo. Fixed in `addd29c`,
`PATCH_VERSION` 22; behaviourally nil at 0.02% against ~5% measurement noise.

### The units are not comparable across heuristics

Both options are a multiple of `nnz << 10` — vanilla HiGHS's own hardcoded
single-thread FeasibilityJump limit — so `effort = 1.0` is one vanilla FJ
budget and `patience < effort` is legible without arithmetic (#116). But what
each heuristic's effort *counter* counts differs: FJ step units, FPR and
LocalMIP coefficient accesses, Scylla PDLP iterations x nnz. `local_mip 13.96`
against `scylla 3.07` says nothing about which searches harder.

**Scope differs too**, and this is why the numbers are valid at 16 workers and
not in general: FJ's pair sizes **one worker's** allowance, the other three
size a **whole dispatch** which `make_budget` then divides by N. Changing the
worker count therefore *reallocates* budget between heuristics rather than
rescaling it.

## What each heuristic's dispatch looks like

| heuristic | dispatches | improvements | finished | still improving | barren | stale effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| fpr | 220 | 730 | 139 | 1 | 80 | 98.5% |

**97–99% of every heuristic's effort lands after its last incumbent
improvement.** That is the direct measurement of unproductive spend this probe
exists to produce, and it is what makes the patience clamp a live constraint
rather than a formality.

## Quality against best known

Not required by #113, but "did it produce" cannot rank candidates. Scored with
`run_target`'s own `presolve_objective` and `primal_gap`, so a candidate cannot
score differently here than in #107.

| heuristic | scored | median gap | mean gap | exact | best of four |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fj | 147 | **0.1102** | 0.3398 | 23 | **96** |
| local_mip | 162 | 0.2015 | 0.4140 | 21 | 88 |
| scylla | 130 | 0.4060 | 0.4955 | 14 | 46 |
| fpr | 138 | 0.6679 | 0.6045 | 10 | 37 |

**FJ leads on median gap and best-of.** On the 2026-08-27 run it was *last*,
with a median gap of 1.0000 — the saturation cap, i.e. never meaningfully
close. That is #139's two upstream FeasibilityJump corrections (the
negative-coefficient jump value, and the inverted sign of the objective term in
the move score) showing an effect far larger than the 25-instance A/B that
justified them suggested. 21 instances have `|reference| <= 1`, where the
capped gap saturates and the ranking falls to cost alone.

## The instance sets

**Informative set: 191 of 233 (82.0%)** — instances where the presolve chain
produced the reported incumbent, as a union over the four arms. Written to
`informative.txt`.

**Hard tier: 42**, in `hard_tier.txt`, each with its reason: 41
`no-acceptance` (no arm produced anything) and 1 `trivial-only` (`ex9`, where
only HiGHS's own trivial heuristics produced). They are **not discarded** —
they are scored separately on *did any configuration crack it*, so a
breakthrough shows up without diluting the quality ranking. On this probe none
did, which is what makes them constants for #107's search.

**Tuning set: 90 instances**, `bench/instances_tuning.txt`, sampled from the
informative set and stratified on **vanilla** time-to-first-feasible — not on
anything this probe measured, which is what keeps the set from being
conditioned on the outcome it will be used to compare. Largest-remainder
proportional allocation in integer arithmetic:

```
<1s 42 | 1-10s 27 | 10-100s 10 | 100-600s 6 | >=600s 1 | never 4
```

90 rather than the old 25 because the winner of a search over `n`
configurations is biased upward by roughly `sigma * sqrt(2 ln n)`; at 25
instances the selection noise of a wide search would have exceeded the signal.

## What moved since 2026-08-27, and why

The previous measurement shipped fj 2.84 / 0.71, fpr 7.672 / 1.918, local_mip
29.232 / 7.308, scylla 1.136 / 0.284. **All four moved, and none of it is
noise** — each has a named defect behind it, which is exactly why the re-run
was held until they had all landed.

* **fj 2.84 -> 0.5665, a factor of 5 down.** The old number rested on **one**
  finished dispatch of 220; this one rests on 144. #163: `FjWorker` offered
  once per attempt, after `solver.solve()` returned, and under this probe's
  configuration one attempt *is* the whole 30 s dispatch — so all 16 workers
  published within a millisecond of the cap, the first incumbent improvement
  arrived at a median of **29.7 s of 30 s**, and the knee was forced to be the
  entire spend. It now offers on each improving solution: first improvement at
  a median of **30 ms**, 10 improvements per instance against 2, 8,141
  observed improvements against 365. **FJ converges fastest of the four**; it
  simply could not say so. Quality is unmoved by the fix (median gap 0.1100 ->
  0.1102), confirming it changed *when* FJ publishes, not what it finds.
* **local_mip 29.232 -> 13.9607, halved.** #162: the deadline poll was paced in
  local-search *steps*, and a feasible-mode step charges one `nnz` every
  `kFeasibleRecheckPeriod` steps, so on a large model a dispatch ran far past
  its limit — measured **+46 s on a 1.4M-nonzero model, at both a 15 s and a
  30 s limit**, the identical residual at both being the signature of a bound
  the clock cannot shorten. The knee is now measured against the wall clock
  the dispatch was actually given.
* **scylla 1.136 -> 3.0680, roughly tripled.** #152: every clock-bound Scylla
  dispatch used to retire at ~half its limit, so its arm of the previous run
  measured a ~15 s cap where the others had 30 s. Confirmed fixed here at 16
  workers — the count at which it had never been verified — with a median
  dispatch end of **30.04 s** against a 30 s cap. #153 and #140 additionally
  moved its charged effort per pump round.
* **fpr 7.672 -> 12.2559.** #124, #156, #130, #131 and #158 all raise charged
  effort per unit of real work on FPR's kernel, and the effort default is a
  quantile on exactly that axis — so a *rise* is the predicted direction.

The residual overruns are the documented floors and nothing else: per-arm
median dispatch end against the 30 s cap is fj 30.01, fpr 30.00, local_mip
30.00, scylla 30.04, with 5 dispatches of 881 past 32 s (FPR and Scylla, one
RepairWalk step and one PDLP solve respectively). The 2026-08-27 run lost **23
runs to #117 kills**; this one lost none, and reported `abandoned_setup=0`
everywhere, so the barren counts that the patience side rests on are honest.

## Caveats that travel with these numbers

* **Valid at 16 workers only**, for the scope reason above.
* **The stale fractions conflate two wastes** — the heuristic being finished,
  and 16 workers redundantly searching after the last improvement. Separating
  them needs a single-worker control, which was not run.
* **One seed.** This is a screen: it asks whether a heuristic can *ever*
  produce on an instance, and the union over four heuristics carries most of
  the diversity a second seed would add. The quantiles are pooled over
  hundreds of dispatches.
* **Only the root dispatch is observed.** The presolve chain is re-entered for
  sub-MIPs during a full solve; a presolve-only run sees the first dispatch
  only. That is the cleanest comparable unit but it is not the whole spend.
* **The two-budget confound was not measured.** #113 asks for a control at a
  bindable budget to show how much the trajectory depends on the budget it was
  taken at. Deferred to the configuration ablation, which re-measures both
  regimes anyway.
* **`effort` is a search centre, not an optimum.** See the bracketing ranges
  above.
